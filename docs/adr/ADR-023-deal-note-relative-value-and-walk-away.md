# ADR-023: The deal note prices relative to the list: median multiple ÷ list median per film, with the weakest film as the walk-away line

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-20
- **Decision owner:** Noell

## Context

Kwame asked what to pay for the strongest franchise relative to everything else, and the one number that drove the view. The brief gives no prices.

## Decision

The number that drives the view is the bundle's median multiple divided by the list median multiple of the same window. The walk-away line is the bundle's weakest film's multiple divided by the same median: pay no more per film than that ratio supports relative to a typical film. Both are ratios, so they hold whatever the prices are. Each window is measured against its own median.

## Alternatives considered

### Alternative A: Relative to the standalone median

- **How it would work:** bundle median ÷ standalone median.
- **Why it is attractive:** speaks directly to "bundle or standalones".
- **Why rejected:** the standalone base is 16 films, 8 from 2000 onward. Kept in the deal-note file for reference.

### Alternative B: Compare against the all-years median in every window

- **How it would work:** one reference figure.
- **Why it is attractive:** simpler.
- **Why rejected:** mixes eras, which is Yaw's objection. Measured effect: Star Wars is 2.49× the all-years median but 1.31× the 2000-onward median within its own later films.

## Consequences

- **What this buys:** The Lord of the Rings: 2.38× the 2000-onward median per film, walk-away 1.90× (weakest: The Fellowship of the Ring).
- **What this costs:** assumes licence value scales with past box-office return (S26).
- **What it costs the client if I am wrong:** overpaying for a bundle whose audience on the service is smaller than its box office suggests.
- **What would make me revisit this:** actual licence prices or viewing data.

## What this survives

The ratios scale to any list size and any price level.
