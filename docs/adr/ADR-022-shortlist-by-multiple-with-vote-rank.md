# ADR-022: The shortlist ranks by multiple, shows each pick's vote rank, and names one audience pick

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-20
- **Decision owner:** Noell

## Context

Kwame asked for three franchise targets and five standalones with a reason for each. Finding F-07: the films with the weakest box-office return include some of the most widely watched.

## Decision

Top 3 rankable bundles and top 5 standalones by multiple, in each window. Each pick shows its vote rank and a plain reason comparing it with that window's list median. One audience pick per window: the most-voted standalone not in the top five.

## Alternatives considered

### Alternative A: Combined rank of multiple and votes

- **How it would work:** average each film's rank on the two measures.
- **Why it is attractive:** balances box office and audience interest.
- **Why rejected:** a new measure with an arbitrary 50/50 weight that the headline does not use; the memo would contradict itself.

## Consequences

- **What this buys:** the shortlist follows from the same measure as the recommendation, and the disagreement with audience interest stays in front of Kwame (audience picks: Interstellar over all years; WALL·E from 2000 onward).
- **What this costs:** a 2000-onward standalone list of 5 drawn from 8 films is barely a selection; only 2 of the 5 clear that window's list median.
- **What it costs the client if I am wrong:** if audience interest matters more than return, the audience picks are the better buys.
- **What would make me revisit this:** StreamVault's own viewing data.

## What this survives

Larger lists make the selection meaningful; the shortlist sizes are settings.
