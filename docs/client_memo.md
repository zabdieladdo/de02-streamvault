## What I found

Of the 127 titles on your list, 112 could be analysed; 10 entries were blanks, repeats or numbers from another system, and 5 matched no film. Figures are as of 20 November 2026.

Franchise films earned more for each dollar spent than standalone films, over all years and from 2000 onward. The typical franchise film returned 5.6 times its budget against 3.8 for standalones; from 2000 onward, 5.1 against 3.8.

**The titles to buy change depending on the years you trust.** Over all years, the strongest franchises are Star Wars, The Lord of the Rings and Indiana Jones, and the strongest standalones are four films from 1966 to 1994 and Spirited Away. From 2000 onward, the period Finance trusts, the list is different, and Star Wars drops from 2.5 to 1.3 times the typical film.

Later films in a franchise earn about 70% of what the first film earned, so the first film carries much of a bundle's value.

## What I recommend

Prioritise franchise bundles. Shortlist, from 2000 onward:

- **Franchises:** The Lord of the Rings, Ice Age, The Avengers.
- **Standalones:** Spirited Away, Inception, Interstellar, Memento, The Departed.

**Deal note: The Lord of the Rings.** Its typical film returned 2.4 times what a typical film on your list returned from 2000 on; that number drove this view. Its weakest film, the first, still returned 1.9 times the typical film, an exception to the pattern above. Pay up to about 1.9 times, per film, what you would pay for a typical title; above that the weakest film no longer supports the price. This assumes price should follow past box-office return.

**Confidence: moderate.** The direction is firm; the titles are less certain, because only 8 standalone films from 2000 onward were available. One film, The Good, the Bad and the Ugly, lifts the standalone average, so I rely on the typical figure.

**What would change this:** your own viewing figures. The two standalones with the lowest return, The Shawshank Redemption and Fight Club, are among the four most voted on by audiences. If viewing, not box office, drives your value, standalones may be worth more than this shows.
